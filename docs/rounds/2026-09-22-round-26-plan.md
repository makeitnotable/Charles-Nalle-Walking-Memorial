*Wil, 2026-09-22, after approving round 25 ("Looks good"): "One small bug
left. It occurs when a user taps a painting and the drawer is shown." His

tagged locally `client-round-26-base` and pushed as
`claude/client-round-26-base`. Revert: `git reset --hard
client-round-26-base`.*

## 1 · Why it appeared now

The drawer (`.museum-sheet`) was designed with an open bottom — no stroke
there, v14.3: "off-screen, a line there reads as a seam" — on the
assumption that its lower edge sits under the toolbar. Until round 24 the
band under the toolbar was Safari's solid fill in the same brown, so the
drawer looked anchored. Round 24 made that band glass over the runway's
bottom strip, and the strip paints the floor, because it only knows the
hall. The drawer lives inside the pinned stage, which stops at the page
area's edge (playbook §1, fact 2), so it cannot reach under the toolbar
itself.

## 2 · What ships

Museum.tsx only, in the frame loop that already paints the strip. Whenever
the drawer covers the stage's bottom edge — its rendered box, which follows
the slide, has its top edge above the stage's bottom — the bottom strip
continues the drawer from the drawer's top edge down instead of the floor:

- the same floor rows, blurred as the drawer's `backdrop-filter` blurs them
  (the rows through a canvas an eighth the size and back — a soft blur of
  about the drawer's 10px, no `filter` dependency);
- the drawer's own ground laid over them — its computed `background-color`,
  read from the element once per mount and checked against the canvas's
  parser (90% page brown, `color(srgb … / 0.9)`), with a fallback of the
  same numbers;
- its side strokes, 1px in the drawer's border colour, at the strip's edges.

The floor returns the frame the drawer leaves. A drawer slid out below the
stage never qualifies (its top edge is not above the stage's bottom); the
desktop card is not a sheet and no bars exist there. Nothing else changes:
the pin, the top strip, the lead cover and every page other than this one
are untouched, and the strip's behaviour without a drawer is byte-identical.
