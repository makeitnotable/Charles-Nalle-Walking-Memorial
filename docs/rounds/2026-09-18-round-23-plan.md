## 1 · The area behind the top bar follows the section

**His words.** "Set the area behind/around the browser's top UI (address bar /
toolbar / safe-area inset region) to a solid fill that matches the background
color of the section currently visible at the top of the viewport. The color
should update as the user scrolls between sections. Example: if the hero
section is currently visible, use the hero section's brown background color."
Three shots, all with the address bar minimized: the painting through the pill
over the hero; the heading ghosting through over the quote; the cream showing
through over the story with a dark band at the very top.

**Diagnosis.** Every chapter route handed Safari one static colour, the hero
brown `#100a06`, for the whole page (round 8 phase E, his instruction then).
Safari uses that colour only as a tint. Once the bar minimizes the layout
viewport starts below the pill (playbook §1: 645 at rest, 753 minimized, the
pair 199), and the region above it — status bar and pill — is glass over what
the document holds ABOVE the viewport's top edge: what has just scrolled past,
under a short gradient of `<body>`'s colour. That is all three shots at once.
The dark band over the cream is the brown gradient. Round 18's last attempt
(hypothesis 2: opaque strips at the edges, on screen, under `<main>`) did not
take — his shots are the result — so it is retired.

**What ships.**

- `<body>` follows the section at the top edge on the chapter routes — the
  round-21 rule from `/map`, gated on `data-top-bar="section"` as well as
  `data-root-fill="map"`. The resolver (`sectionGroundAt`) now counts `<main>`
  (the page ground since round 14) and `.ground-cream` blocks as grounds, and
  falls back to `<main>` before `<body>`: the story transcript and the history
  section paint their cream on a `<div>` inside the `<section>`, and the old
  walk fell past it to `<body>` — so over the cream the answer was the hero's
  brown (a latent bug in the Chrome-for-Android meta as well; fixed for both).
  The static `#100a06` rule stays as the at-rest and no-JS value; at scroll 0
  the sampler writes the same value, so nothing moves at rest.
- The **chapter cover**, `.edge-cover`: a 320px absolute box in the document
  (a child of `<body>`, in the scrolling layer, never fixed), moved on every
  scroll event so its BOTTOM edge rides on the viewport's top edge
  (`translateY = scrollY − 320`), coloured by the sampler with the same
  section colour. It covers only what has scrolled past — the reader sees it
  through the bar and nowhere else — which is the one region in-flow paint
  reaches and fixed paint does not (A2's clip; the map's runway). Its bottom
  16px are feathered by a mask: scrolling is asynchronous on iOS, so a fast
  upward flick can leave the cover a frame behind, and the feather turns a
  hard band into a faint haze under the pill. `display: none` unless
  `-webkit-touch-callout` is supported (the iOS gate the playbook records), so
  desktop, Android and the visual gate never see it and no listener is
  attached there. Z 800: over everything in `<main>`, under the walk rail, the
  mini-player and the menu. Device flags, inline and one page load:
  `?cover=off` (a before/after), `?feather=<px>` (0 = a hard edge).
- The chapter strips (round 18) are gone: the chapter routes no longer force
  any opaque extension at either edge. Nothing is painted behind the bottom
  toolbar — his answer to question 1.

**Residue, reported before shipping.** Safari has one tint for both bars, from
`<body>`. So when the bottom toolbar is expanded it carries the top section's
colour as a cast over whatever is under it — invisible while top and bottom
are in the same section, a faint mismatch across a section boundary. No
page-side lever separates the two bars; the page paints nothing there.

**What only the phone can show.** Whether Safari composites the cover through
the pill the way it composites the runway (the same in-flow mechanism, so it
should); how far a fast flick leaves the cover behind, and whether 16px of
feather hides it; how Safari's own tint animation (~300 ms) reads against the
cover's instant switch at a section boundary.

## 2 · The corner menu holds its place

**His words.** "The menu animates by sliding from the right to the left and
from up to down slightly when the user scrolls. (It's like its position is
getting pushed by the scroll bar.) On scroll up the menu should fade in; on
scroll down the menu should fade out. Do not use a slide-in/translate-from-
right animation for this menu."

**Diagnosis.** It was never animated. `--ui-inset` is one lane for every
corner and takes the deepest of the four safe areas; `env(safe-area-inset-
bottom)` is 0 with the bars expanded and 34px once they collapse (playbook
§1). So the top-right burger jumped from 20px to 34px on both axes — 14px down
and 14px left — on every scroll down and back on every scroll up, in the same
moment its retreat fade ran. The playbook listed this shift as open, by his
decision; this is the decision.

**What ships.** The top-right menu reads its own `--menu-inset`: the gutter,
the top inset and the right inset — its own two edges — and nothing at the
bottom edge can move it. The fade is unchanged (opacity plus the small settle
it already has; his answer to question 3). Every page that shows the menu
places it top-right (the map moved there in round 1), so this is the
site-wide behaviour he asked for. No other corner control changes: the
mini-player and the interlude credit still ride `--ui-inset` (RUN-STATE, open).

## 3 · The hero lockup sits on the gutter

**His words.** "On mobile (and tablet if the bottom toolbar is present), move
the hero section's text on all five chapter pages (excluding only part 2 of
chapter 2) down so that the vertical spacing between the bottom of the chapter
title and the top of the bottom toolbar feels optically the same as the
horizontal spacing between the left of the hero section text elements and the
left edge of the screen."

**Diagnosis.** The hero is one small viewport tall, so at rest its bottom edge
is the toolbar's top. The lockup's inset was `max(5svh, 32px)` — 32px on his
phone — plus the title line box's air under the baseline (Libre Caslon
Display at `--lh-display` 0.95: `(0.95 − 0.970 + 0.266) / 2` = 0.123em, 6px at
the phone size): the eye read ~38px against a 20px gutter.

**What ships.** On phones (below 768) the shell's inset is `var(--gutter)` and
the title's negative bottom margin takes the line-box slack out — ink to ink,
not box to box: on the left the eye measures the gutter plus the capital's
side bearing (~0.05em), so the margin removes 0.123 − 0.05 = 0.073em and the
last line's ink sits the same distance above the edge as the first letter's
ink sits from the left. On tablets (768–1023) the same, but only in a browser
with collapsing bars: `100lvh − 100svh` is the bars' height there and 0 on an
iPad and every desktop, and a trig cast turns that length into a 0/1 switch
(`clamp(0, tan(atan2(100lvh − 100svh, 1px)), 1)`), behind an `@supports` gate
so an older browser keeps the old inset rather than losing it. Desktop (1024
and up) and Commissioner's Part-2 hero (`#hero-2`) are untouched.

## Device pass 2 (2026-09-18, 17:41) — and the third push

Wil, two screenshots (the hero and the history section, bar minimized): "The
background fill behind/around the browser address bar changes height as you
scroll (it looks like it grows/shrinks). It must be a solid, stable fill that
never changes height or size while scrolling." And: "There's a thin gap/slit
above the chapter page indicators (the orange progress bars) where underlying
text shows through. This area must be fully covered by the same solid fill."
In both shots the cover's bottom edge sits 3–6px above the rail, a different
amount in each.

**Diagnosis.** The tracker set the cover's transform from `scrollY` on each
scroll event and never re-read it. The position in the last scroll event is
not where iOS finally settles — the compositor finishes the scroll after the
last event the main thread sees — so at rest the cover was short by the
remainder, a different remainder after every scroll: the "growing and
shrinking". And the cover's edge was aimed at the viewport's top with the
rail's 3px line sitting on it, so any shortfall opened a slit of content
between the two.

**What ships.**

- The cover's bottom edge is defined by the rail: it sits on the rail's bottom
  edge, so its last 3px lie behind the rail's line (z 800 under 900). A
  shortfall of up to 3px cannot show, and the fill ends exactly where the rail
  is, with nothing below it.
- Where the browser supports scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline:
  scroll()`; Safari 26), the compositor moves the cover: a linear animation on
  the root scroll timeline whose range is an absolute 0–100000px of scroll
  offset, so `translateY = offset + rail − 320` at every frame, from the true
  offset, with no main-thread lag and no stale value. The script self-checks
  it on the first scroll events past 60px — the cover's rendered bottom must
  sit on the rail's, or a browser mapping the range differently would show a
  slab sweeping the page — and switches the animation off if it fails.
- The scroll-event tracker stays as the fallback, and behind `?cover=js` on
  the device: it anchors to the rail from the layout viewport's offset
  (`visualViewport.pageTop − offsetTop`, so a visual/layout split cannot open
  a slit) and keeps re-reading on requestAnimationFrame for 600 ms after the
  position last changed, so the settled state is the one painted.

**The hero inset — verified, and why it can look unchanged.** The deployed
build carries the round's rules: at 390 wide the lockup's inset is the gutter
and the title's last line sits 22–23px above the hero's bottom edge, which at
rest is the toolbar's top (the probe's numbers above, on the build Actions
runs 248 and 249 deployed). The lockup also RISES as the page scrolls — the
v4 scrub moves it 60px up over the first 55% of the hero — so in any scrolled
view, including the device-pass-2 hero shot, the gap below the title is 22px
plus up to 60px of that rise, and the toolbar has collapsed by then. The
number to check is at rest: page top, bars expanded, toolbar visible.

**One hazard found on the way, and closed.** The probe's own still-sheet
(the snap gate's: every `animation-duration` forced to 0s) parked the
scroll-driven animation on its END keyframe, 100000px down the page — a
scroll timeline with a 0s duration is instantly finished — and the
self-check duly switched it off. The site carries the same idiom for
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (`animation-duration: 0.01ms !important`,
global.css), so under Reduce Motion the cover would have done the same on a
phone. The animation now applies only under `prefers-reduced-motion:
no-preference`; under `reduce` the rail-anchored tracker drives the cover —
not because the cover is motion (nothing the reader sees moves) but because
that is the only way to be certain of it. And the self-check now runs at rest
too: a failure is visible at any offset, a pass only from 60px.

**Verification.** build + check-css; astro check 0 errors 0 warnings;
qa:scope; the round-23 probe on three paths — the animation, `?cover=js`, and
a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` context — two chapters, 21 depths each:

## Device pass 4 (2026-09-18, 20:14) — and the fifth push

Wil, a crop of the expanded address bar mid-scroll over the archival photo:
"This is what happens when scrolling. It also looks buggy / jittery and not
smooth when the content scrolls through the area behind and around the
address bar. Also the fill still changes size." In the crop the rail sits at
the layout viewport's top (108 CSS px, under the expanded bar) and the
cover's edge ~30px above it, with the photo showing between and blurred

**What the crop proves.** A 30px gap at speed is a frame's worth of scroll.
The cover was being placed from the main thread on his phone — either the
scroll timeline is not accelerated in his Safari, or the script was
driving — and nothing positioned from the main thread can be smooth in the
bar region: the compositor draws that region from the true scroll offset
every frame, and a main-thread box is always one frame behind, by an amount
that varies with speed. That IS the jitter, and it IS the fill changing
size. The only in-flow thing the scrolling thread itself places is
`position: sticky`.

**What ships.** The cover is a sticky box, first in `<body>`'s flow: 320px
tall with a −320px bottom margin (so `<main>` still starts at 0 and the
document is not one pixel taller), drawn 319px higher than it is laid out,
with `top: 0`. From the first pixel of scroll the scrolling thread holds its
layout box on the viewport's top edge, so the painted box always spans the
region above that edge — no script, no timeline, no lag, and its last pixel
inside the rail's line. The script only colours it. The tracked variants
stay behind flags: `?cover=track` (pass 3's scroll timeline, self-checked)
and `?cover=js` (the script); `?cover=off` and `?cover=debug` as before, the
readout now naming `sticky`.

**What only the phone can show.** Whether WebKit paints a stuck box's pixels
in the bar region the way it paints the scrolled runway's. Round 8's A2 saw
a pinned box's *overflow* clipped at the viewport, but that box also carried
`overflow: clip`, which Safari applies without the clip-margin Chrome
honours, so the measurement does not settle it; a sticky box painting its
own bounds is untested. If it does not paint there, the bar shows content
again (the pass-0 look, tint tracking still on) and `?cover=track` restores
pass 3.

**Verification.** build + check-css; astro check; qa:scope; the probe on
four modes (sticky, `?cover=track`, `?cover=js`, reduced motion), two
chapters, 21 depths each: the cover's bottom sits 1px inside the rail's top,

## Device pass 8 (2026-09-19, 16:38) — and the ninth push

Wil, three crops of the Bakery in the cream section, minimized and expanded

**The honest answer to his question.** Because the region behind Safari's
top bar shows scrolled page paint and nothing else — fixed and sticky boxes
are clipped at the viewport, measured on his phone (round 8, device passes 4
and 5). A fill there therefore had to be a piece of the page, moved into
place every frame by script, and script runs a frame behind the compositor:
that frame is the moving edge, in every one of the seven passes, whatever
the aim, the lead or the band. The one thing Safari paints in that region
that is STATIC is the canvas — `<body>`'s colour — and it shows only where
the document has not scrolled past it. So a static fill means a document
that does not scroll.

**What ships — the still document, behind `?scroll=inner`.** In this mode
`<html>` is the viewport's height and clips, and `<main>` is the scroller:
exactly the layout viewport plus `--reader-e` under the bottom toolbar (the
runway's E from /map, so content still shows through the bottom bar's glass,
which he asked to keep), padded by the same E at its end. Above the viewport
there is nothing but canvas, and `<body>` already carries the section at the
top edge (the sampler's `sectionGroundAt(4)`), so the bar region is a solid,
static fill that switches colour at each section — no cover, no visor, no
tracker, nothing to lag. `<main>` is `position: relative` so its own
absolutely positioned descendants (the sr-only spans) stay inside its
scrollable overflow instead of giving the still document one of its own.
Everything that listens for the page scrolling on `window` — the sampler,
the corner menu, the narration's mini player — hears `<main>`'s scroll
re-sent on window (Base.astro); the menu reads `<main>`'s offset for its
direction (Menu.astro); the chapter's three scroll triggers measure against
`<main>` (`ScrollTrigger.defaults({ scroller })`, [chapter].astro); hash
deep links land inside it (`scrollIntoView` finds the nearest scroller).
The default is untouched: without the flag the document scrolls as before,
with pass 7's cover and band.

**The trade, which is his to make.** Safari's bars collapse only when the
DOCUMENT scrolls, so in this mode they stay expanded while reading: the
address bar and the toolbar, 199px of his screen, for the whole chapter.
The other side of the same coin: nothing on the page ever re-lays out or
re-tints because a bar moved — no `dvh` reflow, no safe-area jump, no
tint animation. If he prefers this, it becomes the chapter default on iOS
(the CSS gate is one `@supports` block) and the cover, the visor and the
tracker retire; if he prefers collapsing bars, the moving edge is the cost
