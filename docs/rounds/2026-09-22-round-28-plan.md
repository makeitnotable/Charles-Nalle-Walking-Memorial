# Client round 28 — `/paintings`: the drawer expands on scroll

*Wil, 2026-09-22, after round 26: "It's no longer possible to expand the
drawer by scrolling up." Asked which of three things happens: "All of the
above. The drawer does not slide up when you scroll down. When you scroll
down, you start scrolling through the hall. But you do not know that you
were scrolling through the hall and then when you click the back to hall
button you end up in a completely different place. The drawer should expand
when the user scrolls down after selecting a painting to view it in the
focus view." Base `a9de24f` — the `v2` tip when this shipped: while this
round was being built another session shipped its own round 27 on `v2` (the
map's 1858 lens, based on round 26's tip), so this round was rebased onto
that tip and takes the next number. Tagged locally `client-round-28-base`
and pushed as `claude/client-round-28-base`. Revert: `git reset --hard
client-round-28-base`.*

## 1 · What is happening, and what could be established here

With a painting open (approach mode), a swipe up on his phone pans the
document. The camera holds the painting, so the hall walks on invisibly
under the drawer — v14 E7's exact failure mode, which E7 closed with a lock:
`touch-action: none` on the canvas and the drawer's handle, and a document-
level non-passive `touchmove` listener that prevents the default. Since
round 24's pin that lock no longer holds on his iPhone.

What could be established without the phone: the gesture code is unchanged
since the round-24 base (every changed line of Museum.tsx since `1e5b07d`
greps to the runway, the pin, the lead cover and the drawer's continuation);
in Chromium with touch emulation a swipe on the hall and one on the handle
both expand the drawer with the page unmoved, on the current build; and in
WebKit's own source the UI process decides whether a pan may start from the
event regions of the composited layers under the touch
(`RemoteLayerTreeViews.mm`, `touchActionsForPoint`), regions the web process
paints per layer (`RenderLayerBacking::updateEventRegion`,
`EventRegion::unite`), which nothing in the new structure should empty. Which
WebKit rule now lets the pan through on his phone is not measurable here,
and a fix that depended on guessing it would be another blind push.

## 2 · What ships — the drawer no longer depends on the lock

Museum.tsx only, plus one CSS line.

- **The document's scroll drives the drawer.** With a painting open, every
  scroll event moves the drawer exactly as the swipe does: down (finger up)
  expands, up collapses, a hidden drawer is revealed after 8px, and the
  position is the scroll's distance over the drawer's travel from the run's
  start (the position the previous scroll event saw, so the first step
  counts). 160ms after the last scroll event the drawer snaps to full or
  peek at the halfway line, as the swipe and the wheel do. Where the lock
  does hold — Chromium, desktop, an iOS that honours `touch-action` —
  nothing scrolls and nothing here runs; the pointer and wheel paths are
  untouched.
- **The page cannot leave the hall's band.** With a painting open, any
  scroll that would carry the stage off either end of the wrap is taken
  back the same frame, before the un-pin check (V13-10f), so the stage never
  un-pins under the drawer and approach never ends by accident — a scroll
  of 5000px past the end lands at the band's end, still open.
- **Back returns to the tap.** The document position when the painting was
  opened (after the flush scroll, if one ran) is restored the moment
  approach ends, so "Back to the hall" lands where the reader was.
- **The handle gets no tap tolerance.** The lock let the first 6px of a
  touch on a control through unprevented so iOS keeps the control's click;
  the drawer's handle is a `role=button` whose tap is the pointer path's, so
  it needs no click and now has its first move prevented — where prevention
  holds, a swipe on the handle never starts a pan.
- **The pin no longer declares `pointer-events: none`.** Its only role was
  the pin's empty top over the header at rest, which wants no tap anyway;
  one fewer thing WebKit's touch regions could read into. Nothing visible
  changes.

The pin, the runway, the drawer's continuation and the lead cover are
untouched.

## 4 · Device pass — what to look at

Walk into the hall, tap a painting. Swipe up on the drawer or the hall: the
drawer should rise with the finger and settle open; swipe down: it settles
closed. Hide it with the X, swipe up: it returns. Tap "Back to the hall":
you should be exactly where you tapped. FAIL: the drawer not following a
swipe; the hall visibly moving while a painting is open; Back landing
elsewhere; the page fighting the finger (a snap-back while scrolling means
the band clamp fired — say where in the hall).
