## 1 · What the screenshots show, and why

**His words.** Screenshot 1, before scrolling: "make the area behind and
around the bottom toolbar transparent so it behaves like normal Liquid Glass.
The goal is that users can see more (ideally all) of the painting hall
through/around the toolbar." Screenshot 2, scrolled into the hall with the
bars minimized: the same for "the area behind and around the top address
toolbar". Both: "If full transparency isn't the cleanest implementation, an
acceptable alternative is a subtle gradient or fade-in background fill,
similar to what we're using on the Map page."

**Diagnosis.** One defect, one cause. The corridor stage is `position:
sticky`, one viewport tall, with the WebGL canvas absolutely positioned
inside it (`src/components/Museum.tsx`). Safari 26 clips a pinned box's paint
at the layout viewport, stuck or not — playbook §1 (a fixed bleed of 160px
rendered 21), round 8 (the stage's box reaches ~200px past the toolbar band
at rest and his screenshot shows the band cut at the viewport's edge to the
pixel) and round 23's device pass 5 (a stuck sticky box "now it's
transparent"). Only in-flow page paint reaches the bar regions. What Safari
paints there instead is what lies behind the stage in the document: the page
ground on `<main>` under the toolbar, and above the viewport the slot's empty
region that has scrolled past, under Safari's own tint from `<body>`. All of
it is `#1d1411`, so both regions read as a flat slab with a hard cut.

The map never had this because its shell is ordinary in-flow content, grown
by E below and T above the visible area with the page landed at scroll T.
The "subtle gradient" he sees there is Safari's glass tint over live map
pixels; nothing in the site draws a gradient behind the bars.

**What was tried before.** Round 8 measured the bottom band and closed it as
a platform limit (he accepted it on 9/17); un-sticking the corridor was ruled
out as the page's scroll mechanic. Round 18 built a bottom bleed of the
canvas behind `?glass=1`, never checked on the device, which could not have
painted through the stage. Round 23 solved the chapters with the still
document — bars that never collapse — which his answer (1) rules out here.

## 2 · What ships — the runway strips, behind `?glass=1`

*Superseded by device pass 3 below: the strips were never drawn under the
bars on his phone and are gone. Kept as the record of the attempt.*

The stage keeps everything: its box (`stage.clientHeight` is the composition
basis — FOV, thresholds, the walk's scroll total, the sheet split), its
scroll mechanic, its `overflow: hidden`. Three additions, all inert where
there are no bars:

1. **The bleed.** The canvas renders B rows above and below the stage,
   B = `round(down, min(110px, 100lvh − 100svh), 1px)` — the map's T
   expression, 110 on his phone (bars 107 / 92), 0 wherever lvh == svh.
   `camera.setViewOffset(w, h, 0, −B, w, h + 2B)` keeps the stage's own band
   framed pixel-for-pixel (measured, §3). The stage clips it; nothing shows
   through the stage.
2. **The strips.** Two in-flow `<canvas>` elements in the slot, siblings
   placed BEFORE the stage so its opaque ground and canvas paint over them.
   Every frame, after the render and in the same task (the drawing buffer is
   not preserved past it), the bleed rows are copied into them and they are
   placed by transform:

   | strip | shown while | placed at (viewport px) | rows |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | top | the stage's top edge is at or above the viewport's (stuck, or scrolling out at the end) | −(48 + B) → +48 | 48 padding + B bleed + 48 overlap |
   | bottom | the stage's box reaches svh, and the strip stays inside the slot | svh − 48 → svh + B + 48 | 48 overlap + B bleed + 48 padding |

   svh is the bars-expanded viewport height and holds still (playbook §1);
   the toolbar band exists only in that state. With the bars minimized the
   bottom strip sits behind the stage and below the screen, harmless.
3. **The lag answer.** A strip is page paint moved by the main thread, so
   playbook §3's frame of lag applies. Rather than a visor, geometry: the
   48px overlap on the viewport side is hidden behind the opaque stage and
   the 48px padding past the bar region is the nearest rendered row
   stretched, so a lag in either direction moves the seam inside the overlap
   or the padding and never opens a slit of page ground. A one-frame velocity
   lead (the round-23 estimator, symmetric) centres it. What remains is a
   texture phase of a few pixels in a blurred, dark band — only his phone
   can judge it. The top strip fades in over the first 100px after the stage
   sticks, so the ceiling arrives over the header's tail as a fade, not a
   cut. A paused render loop hides both strips.

**Flags, one page load each, inline in the URL:**

| flag | what it does |
|---|---|
| `?glass=1` | the runway, as above (the default, script-placed) |
| `?glass=1&runway=track` | the strips placed by a scroll-driven animation on the compositor, self-checked at rest only (round 23's open measurement: whether this Safari accelerates `animation-timeline: scroll()`) |
| `?glass=1&runway=off` | the bleed renders, no strip exists — the cost of the taller render target alone |
| `&debug=1` | a readout: driving path, timeline support, B, svh, innerHeight, the stage's and strips' edges, frame interval, velocity and lead |

Off the flag every route is byte-identical (§3). When he approves on the
device, it comes off the flag as the map's T did: drop `[data-glass]` from
the `--museum-b` selector in `global.css` and the `dataset.glass` test in
`museumB()`.

Files: `src/components/Museum.tsx`, `src/styles/global.css`,
`scripts/museum-runway.mjs` (+ `package.json` scripts: `qa:runway`), and the
docs in the manifest. `Base.astro` is untouched: `?glass=1` and `?debug=1`
were already parsed there for rounds 17–23.
