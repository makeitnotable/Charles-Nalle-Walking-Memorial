# Phase 6 — Returning Stakeholder Test

**Reviewer role:** returning stakeholder (approved the original design; had not seen the rebuild)
**Device:** phone, 390×844, live site — https://makeitnotable.github.io/Charles-Nalle-Walking-Memorial
**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Screenshots:** `docs/qa/phase6-stakeholder/` (01–16)

I sat down with my phone expecting to have to be polite. I did not have to be polite.
Below is what I saw, screen by screen, answering the two questions I was asked:
*Do I recognize this immediately as our website?* and *Am I wowed by the improvement?*

---

## 1. Home (`01-home.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** Unqualified. This is our front door. The same deep brown-black
room, "Troy, NY" in that small orange voice at the top, CHARLES stacked over NALLE
in those tall cream letters, "1821 —— 1875" with the little orange rule between the
dates, the soft peach Continue pill, and the one-sentence explanation resting at the
bottom. Every element I approved is where I left it.

**Wowed: YES.** The painting behind the lettering is no longer a murky suggestion —
I can *see* the man now, the arms raised, the brick, and the pale map-lines of Troy
drifting over him like a memory of streets. It's the same idea we approved, executed
with far more conviction. The only new thing is a menu button in the top corner, in
our rust-and-orange style, and it belongs.

## 2. The Map, after arriving and waiting (`02-map-after-8s.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** The dark city, our orange pill markers with the numbered dots —
1 Bakery, 5 Barbershop — the rust menu square sitting in the bottom-right corner
exactly where my thumb remembers it.

**Wowed: YES.** Three things landed in the first eight seconds. First, a quiet label
at the top: "THE WALK · FIVE STOPS · APRIL 27, 1860" — the date is finally *on* the
map, which as a historian I have wanted from the beginning. Second, a dotted trail
now stitches the five stops together, so the rescue reads as a route, not a scatter
of pins. Third, the stops have grown into their proper names — "Commissioner's
Office," "Gilbert Mansion," "Ferry Landing" instead of the shorthand "Bank" and
"Ferry." There are two gentle buttons — "Take the walk" and "See Troy in 1860" —
and a hint that says "DRAG TO EXPLORE · TAP A STOP." The old map made you guess;
this one takes your arm.

## 3. Tapping a stop (`03-map-tap-bakery.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** The pill turns bright orange when chosen — same marker language,
same map.

**Wowed: YES.** This is the moment that made me sit up. The map glides down to the
actual corner — 3rd St and Division St, readable right there — and a card rises from
the bottom with the painting *in full color*, "CHAPTER 1 — Holeur's Fashionable
Bakery," and an arrow inviting you in. You can see the next chapter's card peeking
at the edge, waiting. On the old site the map and the chapters were neighbors; now
they hold hands. An "Overview" button appears so you can step back out. It feels
like a museum docent walking you to the exact spot.

## 4. "See Troy in 1860" (`04-map-1860.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** Same room, same buttons — this is an addition, not a departure.

**Wowed: YES.** The modern streets dim and up comes the old ward map of Troy — the
Hudson lettered down the river, the Third Ward, Mount Ida. For someone who has spent
years with these maps, seeing it offered to every visitor with one tap is a gift.
The button politely becomes "Back to today."
*One question for the team, from the historian's chair:* the caption reads "TROY,
NEW YORK · 1860 — PAINTED BY MARK PRIEST." The image shown looks like a period
survey engraving. If Mark painted a rendition, keep the credit; if this is the
original atlas plate, the credit should name the atlas, not Mark. Please verify —
attribution is the one thing a memorial cannot get wrong.

## 5. Below the map — "Five Stops Through Troy" (`05`, `06-map-cards-*.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** Our type, our brown, our numbered orange badges, painting
thumbnails in warm rounded cards.

**Wowed: YES** — with one small honest wrinkle. The introduction is lovely, and the
sentence telling visitors that four stops carry bronze plaques while the Ferry
Landing "is remembered here on the website" is exactly the kind of honesty I asked
for. The full-color thumbnails make the chapter list feel like a gallery wall.
The wrinkle: because I had tapped the Bakery earlier, its floating card stayed
pinned at the bottom while I scrolled the list, sitting on top of the Barbershop
entry. It never blocked me — but it's a stray hair on an otherwise pressed suit.

## 6. Entering the chapter (`07-chapter-top.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** "CHAPTER" in orange small caps, the numbered dot, HOLEUR'S
FASHIONABLE BAKERY stacked tall and cream, the long arrow drawing the eye downward.
That is our chapter opening, letter for letter.

**Wowed: YES.** But the painting has become a *drawing* — Mark's pen-and-ink study,
all crosshatch and urgency — with a line across it: "PRESS AND HOLD TO BRING THE
PAINTING TO LIFE."

## 7. Press and hold (`09`, `10-press-hold-*.png`)

**Recognize: YES** — it resolves into the very painting we approved as the hero.

**Wowed: YES — this is the best thing on the site.** You hold your thumb on the
sketch and the color blooms up through the ink until the finished oil is looking
back at you. It is the artist's process made into a gesture: the study becomes the
painting under your own hand. I held it, let it fade, and did it again. Twice. Show
this to Mark first; he will be moved.

## 8. The quote, the audio, the story (`08-chapter-scroll-01`, `-02.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** The narrative is word-for-word the text we approved — "But
freedom, as Charles would be reminded, is fragile" is intact, and the opening
drop-cap "As" gives it the feel of a printed book.

**Wowed: YES.** Two additions I did not expect. The Marshal's arrest cry now stands
alone as a pull-quote in pale peach with his name beneath it — chilling, and
correct. And beneath the painting sits an audio player — "CHAPTER 1 · 01:25" — with
a note that you can tap any paragraph to hear it read aloud. For visitors standing
on the actual sidewalk, and for anyone who cannot comfortably read a phone in
sunlight, this is the difference between a website and a memorial. "Section 1/4"
quietly tells you where you are; the old site never did.

## 9. Historical Context (`08-chapter-scroll-04.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** The stacked HISTORICAL CONTEXT heading and our numbered orange
dots, the same three facts — the bakery's corner, Troy's wealth, the Fugitive Slave
Act's cruel arithmetic.

**Wowed: YES.** There is now a period photograph of the actual buildings sitting
above the facts, and the caption "MARK PRIEST · NALLE SERIES" credits the painting
above it. The history section finally *shows* as well as tells.

## 10. "Not All Laws Are Moral" / Make a Difference (`08-chapter-scroll-06`, `-07.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** The moral, in our tall cream capitals, exactly as approved.

**Wowed: YES.** Behind the words, faint pen-sketch figures ghost through the brown —
the ink studies again, used as texture. It makes the moral feel inked into the page
rather than typed onto it. "MAKE A DIFFERENCE" with the ACLU call to action
survives intact, which mattered to several of us.

## 11. Where to Next? (`08-chapter-scroll-08.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** WHERE TO NEXT?, Section 4/4, the mini-map card, Get Directions —
all ours.

**Wowed: YES.** The little map is now tilted, with the buildings of Troy standing up
out of it in relief — you can practically feel the walk to 5 State Street. "Continue
the walk" is the loudest button on the screen, as it should be, and beneath it small
orange doorways to THE PEOPLE OF THIS DAY, THE PAINTINGS, and ABOUT — plus "Share
this chapter." The chapter now ends by handing you somewhere to go.

## 12. The menu (`12-menu-open.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** Warm brown panel, orange entries, the five numbered stops
indented under Home, then The Walk, The People, The Paintings, About — with a
generous X at the bottom where a thumb actually is.

**Wowed: YES.** Modest and correct. It reads like a table of contents in a
well-made book.

## 13. The People (`13-people-top.png`, `14-people-scroll.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** Our colors, our small-caps eyebrow (THE PEOPLE), Harriet
Tubman's cry set as a pull-quote with her name in orange beneath — unmistakably
our house style.

**Wowed: NO — and it pains me, because the idea is wonderful.** The page opens with
its headline visibly broken. It reads, in large cream capitals:
"ONE DAY. A WHOLE CITY'SONE DAY. A WHOLE CITY'S CAST.NBSP;CAST." — the line is
doubled and a stray "NBSP;" sits in the middle of it like a typo carved into a
plaque. Everything below it is genuinely excellent: THE MAN AT THE CENTER / CHARLES
NALLE, THE GENERAL / HARRIET TUBMAN, PETER BALTIMORE with his Underground Railroad
credit, and under each person little pill-links into the exact chapters where they
appear — a cross-index I've wanted since the first meeting. But a visitor meets the
broken headline first. Fix that one line and this page is a YES with a circle
around it.

## 14. The Paintings (`15-paintings-top.png`, `16-paintings-scroll.png`)

**Recognize: YES.** Our gallery: dark walls, warm frames, orange chapter links.

**Wowed: YES.** Ten works, each credited "Mark Priest · Nalle Series," each with a
CHAPTER → link back into the story, and an introduction that honors the years Mark
spent walking these streets. The Commissioner's Office crowd scene in full color on
that dark background stops the scroll. It is a proper retrospective, and it treats
the paintings as the collection they are.

---

## Overall verdicts

**RECOGNIZE: YES** — on every single screen. Nothing has been cheapened, nothing
foreign has crept in. It is our website the way I remember approving it, only
grown up.

**WOWED: YES** — thirteen screens out of fourteen delighted me; the one that did
not (The People) fails only on a single broken headline, not on design.

## My three favorite moments

1. **Press and hold to bring the painting to life** — Mark's ink study blooming
   into the finished oil under my thumb. The artist's process as a gesture. I did
   it twice and then a third time to show no one in particular.
2. **Tapping a stop on the map** — the glide down to the real corner of 3rd &
   Division and the full-color chapter card rising to meet you. The map and the
   story finally hold hands.
3. **The audio player and read-aloud** — "Tap any paragraph to hear it read
   aloud." Standing on Division Street with Kathy's words in your ears: that is
   the walking memorial we described to each other three years ago.

## Before I show the museum board (small list)

- **The People headline** — repair "ONE DAY. A WHOLE CITY'S CAST." (currently
  doubled with a literal "NBSP;" showing). This is the only true blemish I found.
- **1860 map credit** — confirm "PAINTED BY MARK PRIEST" is accurate for the image
  shown; if it is the period atlas plate, credit the atlas.
- **Map card lingering** — after tapping a stop, its floating card stays over the
  chapter list when you scroll down; consider tucking it away on scroll.
